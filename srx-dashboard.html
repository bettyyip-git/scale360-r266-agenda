💡 Highlights & Observations
✅ Improvements & Positive Observations
✅ Strong Improvement: Headline ScaleUp Time
The overall Headline ScaleUp time improved by 14.4% from April (11m 13s) to July (9m 36s), saving 1 minute 37 seconds.

Reason: This improvement is primarily driven by significant gains in non-release scaleups, which make up the majority of scaleup activity (~83% based on release percentage tracking). The consistent month-over-month reduction suggests infrastructure optimizations and improved caching strategies are having a positive impact.

Gap to Target: Still 3m 36s above the 6-minute target, requiring continued focus on optimization efforts.
✅ Significant Improvement: Non-Release ScaleUp Time
Non-release scaleups showed 19.4% improvement from April (10m 23s) to July (8m 22s), reducing time by 2 minutes 1 second.

Reason: Likely due to improved image caching (cache hit rate increased from 56% in April to 81% in July), image download optimizations, and infrastructure improvements. Non-release scaleups benefit more from caching since they don't require new image downloads as frequently.
✅ Improvement: Core App Startup Hyperforce (Preprod)
Preprod startup time improved by 7.6% from April (6m 8s) to July (5m 40s).

Reason: The preprod environment shows more consistent improvement patterns, likely benefiting from staging infrastructure optimizations before they reach production. The reduction suggests code and infrastructure changes are being validated effectively in preprod.
📊 Observation: Image Cache Hit Rate Improvement
Image cache hit rate improved significantly from 56% in April to 81% in July (+45% relative improvement).

This correlates directly with the non-release scaleup improvements and suggests that caching strategies are working effectively. The higher cache hit rate reduces the need for image downloads, which historically take 2-3 minutes per scaleup.
⚠️ Degradations & Areas of Concern
⚠️ Degradation: Release ScaleUp Time
Release scaleups showed 19.3% degradation from April (12m 47s) to July (15m 15s), increasing by 2 minutes 28 seconds.

Reason: Release scaleups are trending upward consistently over the 4-month period. Potential causes include:
  • Increasing image sizes (20.52GB in July vs 19.2GB in April - 6.9% increase)
  • More complex release procedures or additional validation steps
  • Network or infrastructure bottlenecks during release windows
  • Increased number of release-related init containers or startup tasks

Action Required: Investigate release-specific bottlenecks and consider optimizing release image sizes and deployment procedures.

🟢 Path to Green:
  • Propose a joint release-time App Startup SLA metric with the Apex team (ref. M4.2.3 App Startup PTG) to align ownership of the ApexPrecompilation warmer root cause.
  • CASAM 266 POCs to take Apex pre-compilation out of the app-startup critical path (or accelerate it with more efficient instance types).
  • Complete rollout of the AWS SOCI snapshotter (targeted for 266) to lazy-load image layers and cut download time.
  • Complete remaining CDS (Class Data Sharing) dev, testing, and enablement (targeted for 266) — early results show ~30s startup savings.
  • Sustain Keymaker client + madkub-init rollout, already delivering 50–60s of scale-up time savings.
📊 Observation: Image Size Growth
CASAM image size increased from 19.2GB in April to 20.52GB in July (+6.9% growth).

This steady growth in image size may be contributing to the release scaleup degradation and needs monitoring. Consider implementing image size controls or optimization strategies to prevent further growth.

🟢 Path to Green:
  • Roll out the AWS SOCI snapshotter (targeted for 266) so pods lazy-load only the image layers they need instead of pulling the full ~20GB image.
  • Enable CDS (Class Data Sharing) builds running in parallel with autobuild to reduce startup work tied to image contents.
  • Establish per-release image-size tracking and a growth budget/threshold, with alerts when the CASAM main_producer image trends upward.
  • Partner with contributing teams to prune unused dependencies and artifacts from the main_producer image.
